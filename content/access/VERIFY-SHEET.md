## Signed off

- **1. Keep egress while working** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): never leave a door unable to open from the egress side while you work, even for a few minutes; if hardware comes off, prop the door, post someone at it, or leave it unlocked, and tell the building contact. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 Ch. 7.*
- **2. Notify before working** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): tell the building contact before working on doors, and put the account on test if door alarms are monitored. *Source: Industry practice.*
- **3. Fire alarm contractor for the release** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): don't touch or test the fire alarm door release without the fire alarm contractor; put the fire alarm account on test first and follow fire alarm testing rules. *Source: NFPA 72 Ch. 14.*
- **4. Free egress** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): an egress door opens from the egress side without a key, tool, special knowledge, or special effort, whatever the lock does on the secure side. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.5.*
- **5. One releasing operation** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): releasing an egress door takes one operation; a lever plus a separate deadbolt or thumb-turn is two and isn't allowed on most exit doors. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.5.*
- **6. Door hardware 34 to 48 in** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): handles, pulls, latches, locks, and other operating parts on accessible doors are 34 to 48 in above the floor. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; ADA 404.2.7.*
- **7. One hand, 5 lbf** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): door hardware works with one hand without tight grasping, pinching, or twisting of the wrist, and takes no more than 5 lbf to operate. *Source: ADA 309.4.*
- **8. Interior door opening force 5 lbf** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): interior hinged doors open with no more than 5 lbf; fire doors use the minimum the AHJ allows. *Source: ADA 404.2.9.*
- **9. Reader reach range 15 to 48 in** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): card readers and keypads are within the 15 to 48 in reach range with clear floor space in front; many specs use 42 to 48 in to the center. *Source: ADA 308.*
- **10. Panic hardware** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): panic hardware is required on exit doors serving assembly and educational occupancies with 50 or more people, high-hazard occupancies, and certain electrical rooms; its actuating portion extends at least half the door width. *Source: IBC Ch. 10.*
- **11. Sensor release requirements** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): a sensor-release maglock door has an egress-side sensor that unlocks it on approach (loss of sensor power unlocks), loss of lock power unlocks, a manual PUSH TO EXIT button, fire alarm or sprinkler release, and a UL 294 listed locking system. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.2.*
- **12. PUSH TO EXIT button** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): the button is labeled PUSH TO EXIT, mounted 40 to 48 in above the floor and within 5 ft of the door, cuts lock power directly (independent of the access control electronics), and keeps the door unlocked at least 30 seconds. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.2.*
- **13. Electromagnetically locked egress doors** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): on this path, hardware on the door with an obvious one-hand operation directly cuts maglock power and unlocks the door immediately; loss of power unlocks; required panic hardware also releases it; UL 294 listed. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.*
- **14. Delayed egress where allowed** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): delayed egress locks only in the occupancies the code allows, in buildings with a complete sprinkler system or complete fire detection system, and generally no more than one delayed egress door in the path to an exit. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1.*
- **15. Delayed egress timing** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): a force of no more than 15 lbf for no more than 3 seconds starts an irreversible release; the door unlocks within 15 seconds (30 if the AHJ approves); a local audible signal sounds; it rearms only manually at the door. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1.*
- **16. Delayed egress releases** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): delayed egress locks release immediately on sprinkler or fire detection activation, on loss of power, and by a signal from the fire command center where there is one. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1.*
- **17. Delayed egress sign** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): a sign above and within 12 in of the release hardware reads "PUSH UNTIL ALARM SOUNDS. DOOR CAN BE OPENED IN 15 SECONDS." (30 where approved), with letters at least 1 in high and 1/8 in stroke. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1.*
- **18. Stairway re-entry** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): stairway doors in multistory buildings generally allow re-entry; where locked from the stair side, they unlock automatically on fire alarm and from the fire command center where there is one; rules depend on building height and code edition. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.5.*
- **19. Annual door inspection** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): fire door assemblies are inspected and tested every year by a qualified person with a written record; NFPA 101 also requires annual inspection of certain egress doors, including doors with panic hardware and electrically controlled egress. *Source: NFPA 80 Ch. 5; NFPA 101 7.2.1.15.*
- **20. UL 294 listing** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): access control system units (controllers, readers, electric locks, power supplies) and electrically locked egress door locking systems are listed to UL 294. *Source: UL 294.*
- **21. UL 294 standby power levels** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): UL 294 standby power levels: Level I none, Level II 30 minutes, Level III 2 hours, Level IV 4 hours; the specification or customer picks the level. *Source: UL 294.*
- **22. Lock standards** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): UL 1034 covers burglary-resistant electric locking mechanisms; the BHMA A156 series grades electric strikes (A156.31), electromagnetic locks (A156.23), and exit devices (A156.3). *Source: UL 1034; BHMA A156.*
- **23. Fire alarm release of fail-safe locks** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): where the building has a fire alarm or sprinkler system, fail-safe locks on egress doors unlock on fire alarm activation and stay unlocked until the fire alarm is reset; the fire alarm contact goes in the lock power path (the power supply's fire alarm input), not through a controller input and software. *Source: IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 72 Ch. 21.*
- **24. Fire alarm relay within 3 ft** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): the fire alarm relay or control module goes within 3 ft of the device it controls, with the fire alarm wiring to the relay supervised (same rule as the signed-off fire pack item). *Source: NFPA 72 Ch. 21.*
- **25. Test the fire alarm release** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): test the fire alarm release on every fail-safe egress door at acceptance and after any change to either system, with the fire alarm account on test: activate, confirm each door releases, reset, confirm each relocks; record it. *Source: NFPA 72 Ch. 14.*
- **26. Fire doors latch; strikes fail-secure** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): fire-rated doors must positively latch, so electrified hardware on them must be listed for fire doors and electric strikes on them must be fail-secure. *Source: NFPA 80.*
- **27. No field modification of fire doors** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): don't cut, drill, or enlarge preps on a fire door or frame beyond what NFPA 80 allows; other modifications need the manufacturer or a listing agency, or the label is void. *Source: NFPA 80 Ch. 4.*
- **28. Firestop rated penetrations** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): seal every cable penetration through a fire-rated wall or floor with a listed firestop system for that assembly. *Source: IBC Ch. 7; NEC 300.21.*
- **29. Class 2 power limits** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): most access power is Class 2: no more than 30 V and 100 VA per output at the voltages used, from a listed Class 2 source. *Source: NEC 725; Ch. 9 Table 11.*
- **30. Class 2 separated from power** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): Class 2 wiring isn't in the same raceway, box, or enclosure compartment as 120 V wiring unless a listed barrier separates them. *Source: NEC 725.136.*
- **31. Cable ratings** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): CL2/CL3 general use, CL2R/CL3R risers, CL2P/CL3P plenums; communications cable (CM, CMR, CMP) of the same or higher level may substitute. *Source: NEC 725 / 722.*
- **32. Cable support** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): cable is supported by the building structure with listed hardware, not laid on ceiling tiles or tied to ceiling grid wires, pipes, or conduit. *Source: NEC 725.24; 300.11.*
- **33. Ethernet 100 m** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): an Ethernet (Category 5e/6) channel to an IP controller or PoE device is limited to 100 m (328 ft). *Source: TIA-568.*
- **34. Shield grounded at one end** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): ground the reader cable shield drain at the controller end only; cut back and tape it at the reader. *Source: Manufacturer manuals.*
- **35. Wiegand wire colors** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): red + power, black ground, green D0, white D1, brown or orange LED, yellow beeper, drain shield. *Source: Manufacturer convention.*
- **36. 26-bit format** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): bit 1 even parity, bits 2 to 9 facility code (0 to 255), bits 10 to 25 card number (0 to 65,535), bit 26 odd parity. *Source: 26-bit Wiegand format.*
- **37. Wiegand 500 ft** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): Wiegand reader runs are good to about 500 ft on 22 AWG shielded cable. *Source: SIA AC-01; manufacturer manuals.*
- **38. OSDP wiring** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): OSDP uses RS-485 (two data wires plus power and ground, twisted pair for data), is two-way and supervised, and multi-drops readers as a daisy chain with each reader on its own address. *Source: SIA OSDP.*
- **39. OSDP 4,000 ft** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): OSDP over RS-485 is good to about 4,000 ft with proper twisted pair cable. *Source: SIA OSDP; TIA-485.*
- **40. OSDP termination** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): long RS-485 runs get a 120 Ω terminating resistor at each end of the bus; short runs often don't need it; follow the controller manual. *Source: TIA-485; manufacturer manuals.*
- **41. OSDP Secure Channel** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): OSDP Secure Channel encrypts the reader link with AES-128; without it OSDP is supervised but not encrypted. *Source: SIA OSDP.*
- **42. Typical lock currents** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): typical planning values: maglocks (600 and 1,200 lb) about 500 mA at 12 V or 250 mA at 24 V, continuous while locked; electric strikes about 200 to 450 mA at 12 V, only while unlocked; electrified locksets about 250 to 500 mA at 12 V; readers 50 to 250 mA; motion REX 15 to 30 mA. Always use the spec sheet. *Source: Typical spec sheets.*
- **43. Solenoid exit device inrush** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): solenoid electric latch retraction exit devices draw an inrush of several amps for a fraction of a second and need a power supply or controller made for it; motorized latch retraction draws much less. *Source: Manufacturer instructions.*
- **44. Suppression diode** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): on DC locks without built-in suppression, put a 1N4001 to 1N4007 diode across the lock terminals at the lock, reverse biased (band to +); AC locks get a MOV instead. *Source: Manufacturer instructions.*
- **45. Power supply listing** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): use a power supply listed for access control (UL 294) or burglar alarm (UL 603) use, and for fire alarm use where it's part of a fire alarm system. *Source: UL 294; UL 603.*
- **46. Load to 80%** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): design an access power supply for no more than about 80% of its rated output. *Source: Design practice.*
- **47. Battery formula and 1.2 factor** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): Required Ah = load current in A × standby hours × 1.2, the same 20% margin used in the signed-off intrusion and fire packs. *Source: Industry practice.*
- **48. Battery replacement** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): sealed lead-acid batteries are commonly replaced every 3 to 5 years and dated when installed. *Source: Battery manufacturers.*
- **49. PoE power** (signed off by David 2026-10-04): standard PoE (802.3af) supplies about 15.4 W per port with about 12.95 W reaching the device; PoE+ (802.3at) supplies 30 W with 25.5 W at the device. *Source: IEEE 802.3af/at.*
