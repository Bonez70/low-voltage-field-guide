## Safety steps

### 1. Keep egress while working
**Where:** Module 1, Lesson 1.5 "Safety and the life safety mindset"
> - **Never leave a door unable to open from the egress side**, even for a few minutes while you work. If you have to take hardware off, prop the door, post someone at it, or leave it unlocked, and tell the building contact. **⟦#1⟧**

**Where:** Troubleshooting introduction
> **Every guide starts the same way:** tell the building contact what you're doing, put monitored door alarms on test, and make sure the door you're working on can always be opened from the egress side while you work. ⟦#2⟧ **⟦#1⟧** Anything that touches the fire alarm release gets the fire alarm contractor and the fire alarm account on test first. ⟦#3⟧

**Proposed:** never leave a door unable to open from the egress side while you work, even for a few minutes; if hardware comes off, prop the door, post someone at it, or leave it unlocked, and tell the building contact.

**Source:** follows from the free egress rule (IBC Ch. 10, NFPA 101 Ch. 7); standard industry practice.

**Shown in the app as:** (IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 Ch. 7)

### 2. Notify before working
**Where:** Module 1, Lesson 1.5 "Safety and the life safety mindset"
> - **Tell the building before you work.** People may get locked out, doors may go unlocked, and forced and held-open alarms may report to a monitoring center. Put the account on test if door alarms are monitored. **⟦#2⟧**

**Where:** Troubleshooting introduction
> **Every guide starts the same way:** tell the building contact what you're doing, put monitored door alarms on test, and make sure the door you're working on can always be opened from the egress side while you work. **⟦#2⟧** ⟦#1⟧ Anything that touches the fire alarm release gets the fire alarm contractor and the fire alarm account on test first. ⟦#3⟧

**Proposed:** tell the building contact before working on doors, and put the account on test if door alarms are monitored.

**Source:** standard industry practice (monitoring center and customer procedures). No code section.

**Shown in the app as:** (Industry practice)

### 3. Fire alarm contractor for the release
**Where:** Module 1, Lesson 1.5 "Safety and the life safety mindset"
> - **Don't touch the fire alarm release without the fire alarm contractor.** The connection that unlocks doors on a fire alarm belongs to both systems. Testing it means putting the fire alarm account on test and following its testing rules (see the Fire pack's testing module). **⟦#3⟧**

**Where:** Reference card "Fire alarm release"
> - The fire alarm contractor owns the relay; coordinate and test together. **⟦#3⟧**

**Also in:** Troubleshooting introduction; Troubleshooting Guide 10 "Doors didn't release on fire alarm (or won't relock after reset)"

**Proposed:** don't touch or test the fire alarm door release without the fire alarm contractor; put the fire alarm account on test first and follow fire alarm testing rules.

**Source:** NFPA 72 Ch. 14 (testing of emergency control functions, notification before testing); industry practice on who owns the relay.

**Shown in the app as:** (NFPA 72 Ch. 14)

## Egress and door codes

### 4. Free egress
**Where:** Module 1, Lesson 1.3 "Fail-safe and fail-secure"
> **The egress side is always free.** On a properly installed door, someone inside can always get out with one motion, whatever the lock does on the secure side. A fail-secure strike stays locked from the outside during a power failure, but the lever or panic bar on the inside still opens the door mechanically. **⟦#4⟧**

**Where:** Reference card "Fail-safe vs fail-secure"
> - Describes the **secure side** only. The egress side is free either way on a properly installed door. **⟦#4⟧**

**Also in:** Module 8, Lesson 8.2 "Free egress, one operation, and hardware"; Reference card "Egress door rules"

**Proposed:** an egress door opens from the egress side without a key, tool, special knowledge, or special effort, whatever the lock does on the secure side.

**Source:** IBC Ch. 10 (door operations, locks and latches); NFPA 101 7.2.1.5.

**Shown in the app as:** (IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.5)

### 5. One releasing operation
**Where:** Module 8, Lesson 8.2 "Free egress, one operation, and hardware"
> - **One operation:** releasing the door takes **one** motion. A lever plus a separate deadbolt or a thumb-turn is two operations, which isn't allowed on most exit doors. **⟦#5⟧**

**Where:** Reference card "Egress door rules"
> - One releasing operation. **⟦#5⟧**

**Proposed:** releasing an egress door takes one operation; a lever plus a separate deadbolt or thumb-turn is two and isn't allowed on most exit doors.

**Source:** IBC Ch. 10 (unlatching); NFPA 101 7.2.1.5. Both have limited exceptions (some dwelling units, existing doors).

**Shown in the app as:** (IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.5)

### 11. Sensor release requirements
**Where:** Module 8, Lesson 8.3 "Sensor release (maglock with motion and push button)"

**Source:** IBC Ch. 10 (sensor release of electrically locked egress doors; "access-controlled egress doors" in older editions); NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.2.

### 14. Delayed egress where allowed
**Where:** Module 8, Lesson 8.5 "Delayed egress and stairway doors"
> **Delayed egress locks** hold an exit door locked for a short delay when someone pushes on it, sounding a local alarm. They're used to stop theft and patient or resident wandering. Only allowed in certain occupancies, and only where the building has a **complete sprinkler system or fire detection system**. **⟦#14⟧**

**Where:** Reference card "Delayed egress"
> - Only in occupancies the code allows, in fully sprinklered or fully detected buildings. **⟦#14⟧**

**Proposed:** delayed egress locks only in the occupancies the code allows, in buildings with a complete sprinkler system or complete fire detection system, and generally no more than one delayed egress door in the path to an exit.

**Source:** IBC Ch. 10 (delayed egress); NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1. Healthcare and detention occupancies have their own allowances.

**Shown in the app as:** (IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1)

### 15. Delayed egress timing
**Where:** Module 8, Lesson 8.5 "Delayed egress and stairway doors"

**Where:** Reference card "Delayed egress"

**Source:** IBC Ch. 10 (delayed egress); NFPA 101 7.2.1.6.1.

## Fire alarm interface and fire doors

### 23. Fire alarm release of fail-safe locks
**Where:** Module 6, Lesson 6.4 "Fire alarm release"
> Where a building has a fire alarm system, **fail-safe locks on egress doors unlock when the fire alarm activates**, and stay unlocked until the fire alarm is reset. **⟦#23⟧**

**Where:** Reference card "Maglock egress: two code paths"
> - Fire alarm or sprinkler activation unlocks until reset **⟦#23⟧**

**Also in:** Module 8, Lesson 8.3 "Sensor release (maglock with motion and push button)"; Reference card "Fire alarm release"; Troubleshooting Guide 8 "Door won't relock, or stays unlocked"; Troubleshooting Guide 10 "Doors didn't release on fire alarm (or won't relock after reset)"

**Proposed:** where the building has a fire alarm or sprinkler system, fail-safe locks on egress doors unlock on fire alarm activation and stay unlocked until the fire alarm is reset; the fire alarm contact goes in the lock power path (the power supply's fire alarm input), not through a controller input and software.

**Source:** IBC Ch. 10 (sensor release, electromagnetically locked doors) and NFPA 101 7.2.1.6 for the release; NFPA 72 Ch. 21 (emergency control functions). Wiring it in the lock power path is industry practice that follows the "independent of the access control electronics" wording.

**Shown in the app as:** (IBC Ch. 10; NFPA 72 Ch. 21)

**Where:** Reference card "Fire alarm release"

**Source:** NFPA 72 Ch. 21 (emergency control function interfaces). Matches fire pack item relay-3ft.

**Shown in the app as:** (NFPA 72 Ch. 21)

### 42. Typical lock currents
**Where:** Module 1, Lesson 1.4 "Power and wiring basics for access"
> - A magnetic lock commonly draws about **0.5 A at 12 V** (half that at 24 V), all the time it's locked. An electric strike draws a few hundred milliamps, only while unlocked. **⟦#42⟧**

**Where:** Reference card "Typical lock and device current"

**Where:** Module 5, Lesson 5.3 "Suppression and kickback"

**Where:** Reference card "Suppression diode"

**Proposed:** on DC locks without built-in suppression, put a 1N4001 to 1N4007 diode across the lock terminals at the lock, reverse biased (band to +); AC locks get a MOV instead.
