# Module 7: Installation and Device Placement

**You'll be able to:** read a fire alarm drawing, place smoke and heat detectors correctly, install duct detectors, wire elevator and HVAC control functions, and protect devices during construction.

---

## Lesson 7.1: Working from the drawings

Fire alarm work starts with **approved drawings**: the plans the AHJ reviewed. They show every device, its type and setting (candela, temperature, address), the circuits, the panel location, and a **sequence of operations** (what happens when each device activates).

- **Install what's on the drawings.** If a device can't go where it's shown (a duct, a beam, a light fixture in the way), get the change approved by the designer. Small moves can change coverage and break the approval.
- **Keep a set of red-line drawings** on site. Mark every change, the real wire routes, and device addresses. These become the **as-built** (record) drawings.
- Check the **edition of NFPA 72** and local amendments listed on the drawings.

---

## Lesson 7.2: Smoke detector placement

These are the basic rules for **spot-type smoke detectors on smooth, flat ceilings**. Beams, joists, sloped ceilings, and high ceilings change the rules. Follow the drawings.

- **Spacing:** a nominal **30 ft** spacing between detectors, and no more than **15 ft** from a wall. [SRC:smoke-spacing]
- **Every point on the ceiling** must be within **21 ft** of a detector (0.7 × the 30 ft spacing). This matters in odd-shaped rooms and long narrow corridors. [SRC:smoke-spacing]
- **Ceiling or wall:** on the ceiling, or on a sidewall with the **top of the detector within 12 inches** of the ceiling. [SRC:smoke-mount]
- **Air movement:** keep detectors at least **3 ft from supply air diffusers** and return air openings. Air blowing across a detector keeps smoke out of it, and dust from a supply vent makes it dirty fast. [SRC:smoke-hvac]
- **Not in a dead-air spot:** avoid the very peak of a pitched ceiling and the corner where wall meets ceiling, per the device instructions.
- **Access:** leave room to service and test it. A detector in an unreachable spot won't get tested.

Residential smoke **alarms** (stand-alone, household) follow the household chapter of NFPA 72, which keeps a **4-inch** rule: on the ceiling at least 4 inches from the wall, or on the wall 4 to 12 inches below the ceiling. [SRC:smoke-mount]

## Lesson 7.3: Heat detector placement

- Use the **listed spacing** for that detector (on the label or instructions), for example 50 × 50 ft. [SRC:heat-spacing]
- **Reduce spacing for high ceilings:** the listed spacing assumes a ceiling about 10 ft high; above that, a reduction factor applies. [SRC:heat-spacing]
- Every point on the ceiling within **0.7 × the listed spacing** of a detector, as for smoke. [SRC:heat-spacing]
- Choose the temperature rating for the hottest normal ceiling temperature (Module 3).

## Lesson 7.4: Duct smoke detectors

Duct detectors stop an air handler from **spreading smoke** through the building. They don't replace area smoke detectors.

- **Where required:** NFPA 90A and the mechanical code call for them on air handlers over a certain size. A commonly cited trigger is supply systems over **2,000 CFM**, detecting downstream of the filters. Larger systems (commonly over **15,000 CFM**) may also need return-side detection. Follow the mechanical drawings. [SRC:duct-cfm]
- **Signal type:** a duct detector is usually set up as a **supervisory** signal that shuts down the fan, rather than a building-wide alarm, unless the AHJ or design calls for an alarm. [SRC:duct-supervisory]
- **Installation:** sampling tube across the duct with holes facing the airflow, in a straight section where air isn't turbulent (often 6 to 10 duct widths from bends or dampers, per the instructions). Check the duct's air velocity is within the detector's listed range.
- **Remote test station or indicator:** required where the detector isn't easy to see and reach, so it can be tested and its alarm LED seen from the floor. [SRC:duct-remote]
- **Test** it with the manufacturer's method (smoke through the test port, or a magnet test for function only, plus an airflow check across the sampling tubes).

## Lesson 7.5: Control functions: elevators, HVAC, doors

The fire alarm often controls other systems. These are installed per the **sequence of operations** and tested with the other trades.

**Elevator recall (Phase I):**
- Smoke detectors in each elevator lobby, the machine room, and the hoistway (when required) recall elevators to the main floor, or to an alternate floor if the main lobby detector is the one in alarm. [SRC:elevator-recall]
- **Shunt trip:** where sprinklers are in an elevator machine room or hoistway, **heat detectors** within **2 ft of each sprinkler head**, with a lower temperature rating and faster response than the sprinkler, disconnect elevator power **before** water flows. [SRC:elevator-shunt]
- The fire alarm provides **dry contacts** to the elevator controller; the elevator contractor wires their side. Test together.

**Control relays:** relays or control modules for emergency control functions (HVAC shutdown, elevator recall, door release) go within **3 ft** of the controlled device, with the wiring to the relay supervised. [SRC:relay-3ft]

**HVAC shutdown:** the fire alarm relay stops the air handler (and closes smoke dampers where the design calls for it) per the sequence of operations.

**Door holders and locks:** magnetic door holders release fire doors on alarm. Electrically locked doors on egress routes unlock on alarm per the building code. Coordinate with the door hardware installer.

## Lesson 7.6: Panels, annunciators, and construction protection

- **Panel location:** where the drawings show, usually near the main entrance or a fire command center, with working space in front.
- **Annunciator:** at the entrance firefighters will use, as approved by the AHJ.
- **Label everything:** circuits in the panel, device addresses on the devices (where allowed), and a zone or point list in the panel.
- **Protect detectors during construction:** smoke detectors installed before construction cleaning is finished get filled with drywall dust. Don't install heads until the area is clean, or cover them with the manufacturer's dust covers and **remove every cover** before the system goes into service. A covered detector can't see smoke. [SRC:construction-dust]
