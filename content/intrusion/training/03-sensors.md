# Module 3: Sensors and Devices

**You'll be able to:** choose the right sensor for each opening or area, explain how each one detects, and avoid the placement mistakes that cause false alarms.

---

## Lesson 3.1: Door and window contacts

A contact is a **reed switch** plus a **magnet**. The switch mounts on the frame, the magnet on the moving door or window. When they're together, the switch is closed (on a normally closed contact). Separate them and it opens.

| Type | Where it's used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** | Doors and windows where you can't drill | Easy to install, visible, can be knocked off |
| **Recessed** (plunger-style bore) | Wood doors and windows | Hidden, durable; drill frame and door in line with each other |
| **Overhead door** | Garage and roll-up doors | Heavy-duty, armored cable, wide gap; floor mount or track mount |
| **Wide-gap** | Loose-fitting doors, steel doors | Stronger magnet allows a bigger gap |

**Gap** is the distance at which the switch opens. Typical surface contacts operate around 3/4" to 1" gap; overhead door contacts are rated for 1.5" to 2" or more. **Steel doors and frames reduce gap**, sometimes by half; use contacts rated for steel.

**Common mistakes**
- Magnet and switch misaligned, so the zone flickers when the door rattles in wind
- Recessed holes not lined up, so the door has to slam to close the zone
- Using a standard contact on a steel door

## Lesson 3.2: Motion detectors (PIR)

A **passive infrared (PIR)** detector doesn't send anything out. It watches for a **change in infrared heat** across its zones. A body-temperature object moving from one detection zone to the next triggers it.

**Key point:** PIRs are most sensitive to motion **across** the pattern, and least sensitive to someone walking straight **toward** the detector. Aim them so an intruder would cross the coverage.

**Mounting**
- Most wall-mounted PIRs are designed for a specific height, commonly about 7 to 7.5 feet. Mounting at the wrong height shrinks coverage or creates dead zones.
- Corner mounting gives the widest coverage of a room.
- Ceiling-mount 360° PIRs are good for open areas and rooms with lots of obstructions.

**Avoid aiming at** (these cause false alarms)
- Heat sources: furnaces, space heaters, fireplaces, wood stoves
- Air vents blowing directly on the detector
- Windows getting direct sunlight
- Moving objects: ceiling fans, curtains near vents, balloons
- Stairs that a large dog can climb (even with pet-immune models)

**Pet immunity** lets animals up to a rated weight (often 40 to 80 lb) move without triggering, but only when mounted exactly as the manufacturer specifies, and only if pets can't climb above the lower detection zone.

## Lesson 3.3: Dual-technology motion

A **dual-tech** detector combines PIR with **microwave** (which bounces radio waves and senses motion by Doppler shift). Both technologies must trip at the same time to alarm.

- Much more resistant to false alarms in tough environments (garages, warehouses, rooms with heat sources)
- Microwave goes through glass and thin walls, so aim it carefully and adjust its range so it doesn't see outside
- Draws more current than a plain PIR, so account for it in your battery calculation

## Lesson 3.4: Glass break detectors

An **acoustic glass break** detector listens for the sound signature of glass breaking: typically a low-frequency "flex" (the thud of the impact) followed by a high-frequency shatter.

- Covers several windows in a room at once, often up to about 20 to 25 feet
- Mount on a wall or ceiling with a clear line of sight to the glass
- Heavy drapes or shutters block the sound; plan for them
- Test with a **glass break simulator** made for that detector, not by clapping or banging
- Doesn't detect a window that's opened or a pane removed quietly; pair it with contacts or motion

**Shock (vibration) sensors** mount on the glass or frame and detect the impact itself. Useful on single panes or where a room is noisy.

## Lesson 3.5: Smoke and heat on a burglar panel

Many residential panels also accept smoke and heat detectors. Key differences from intrusion devices:

- Use devices listed for that specific panel (4-wire smoke detectors need a resettable power output; 2-wire smokes need a compatible zone)
- Fire zones are 24-hour and are always active, armed or not
- Fire signals get priority at the central station
- Residential fire installed on a burglar panel still has code requirements (NFPA 72 chapter on household signaling)

Full fire alarm coverage comes in the Fire pack later. For now: if it's fire, follow the panel manual exactly and check with your supervisor.

## Lesson 3.6: Panic, hold-up, and environmental

| Device | What it does | Zone type |
|---|---|---|
| **Panic button** | User presses for help; audible or silent | 24-hour audible or silent panic |
| **Hold-up / duress button** | Silent robbery alarm at a business counter | 24-hour silent hold-up |
| **Medical pendant** | Wireless button for medical emergency | 24-hour medical |
| **Water / flood sensor** | Detects water on the floor | 24-hour supervisory or auxiliary |
| **Low temperature** | Freeze warning in vacation homes | 24-hour supervisory |
| **CO detector** | Carbon monoxide | 24-hour CO (use listed devices) |

Hold-up buttons must be placed where staff can reach them without being noticed, and the signal must be silent. Make sure the customer knows the difference between panic and hold-up.
